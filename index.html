How We Partner

A structured four-step partnership model.

Every Envision partnership moves through a disciplined sequence. No step is skipped. No partner commits before the commercial structure is transparent and the capability fit is validated.

01
Capability Alignment

Map the fit before committing.

Review partner product lines and identify where Envision's manufacturing, configuration, innovation lab, or federal channel access adds measurable value. Capability alignment is where both sides decide whether a partnership is worth designing.

02
Channel Routing Design

Design the route, not just the product.

Define which opportunities route through Envision TLS, which go through partner GSA lanes, and which are best pursued jointly. Buyer-preference led, not channel-preference led. We do not force TLS where GSA is already converting.

03
Workshare Structure

Formalize labor, OEM, and margin.

Formalize Envision's labor content, OEM or license relationship, and pricing structure. Transparent disclosed-margin model throughout. Every commercial layer is visible to the partner before execution begins.

04
Execution & Scale

Pilot, retrofit, repeat. Scale proven plays.

Execute, measure, iterate. Evaluate long-horizon opportunities like AbilityOne Procurement List pathway only after demand is proven, product is standardized, and labor content is meaningful. No moat is forced before the market earns it.

Strategic Breakdown

The joint opportunity, in detail.

The remainder of this brief outlines a proposed collaborative revenue strategy, channel routing framework, multi-phase commercialization pipeline, and a ranked federal pursuit map. Contents are confidential to the partner review.

The Wedge

AKUA is the fastest realistic near-term revenue line.

The Lange portfolio separates naturally into two commercialization tracks: higher-dollar, longer-cycle structures (RedGuard, CoverSix, Armoda) and a lower-dollar, faster-moving smart-asset product family centered on AKUA. AKUA is where near-term revenue gets proven, not where the long-horizon moat is built.

Immediate wedge
Repeat buys, pilots, and retrofits, not one-off sales.
AKUA supports pilot deployments, small fleet fieldings, and retrofit programs. This is a repeatable product dynamic, not a bespoke engineering effort. The right channel activity here is commercial discipline, not invention.
Honest channel truth
TLS is a useful alternate lane, not a guaranteed sales multiplier.
TLS creates incremental revenue only when it reaches buyers who were not already converting through CoverSix's GSA MAS, or when Envision materially improves access, execution, or buyer confidence. We will not oversell TLS as a demand-creation tool.
Standardize what already exists
Make the existing AKUA upgrade easier to attach.
AKUA is already a collaborative upgrade on relevant Lange assets. The scaling work is not product invention. It is standardizing quote language, ordering logic, retrofit packaging, and route-to-contract decisions so the seller does not have to reinvent the story on every deal.
Long-horizon moat sits later
The AbilityOne Procurement List pathway is phase 5, not phase 1.
The durable federal advantage comes from eventual AbilityOne product-addition for a standardized smart-asset kit. That requires proven demand, defined product, meaningful Envision labor content, and partner alignment. It is a 12 to 24 month disciplined effort, not a near-term sales tool.
Channel Routing

Use the channel that materially improves buyer conversion.

TLS and GSA MAS are not substitutes. Each works for different buyers and different acquisition profiles. Routing decisions should be made on buyer preference and execution fit, not on a generic assumption that one lane is always stronger.

Envision TLS
DLA-managed acquisition lane
  • Buyer experienceDLA-managed acquisition. Buyer submits unfunded requirement. DLA performs scope review and issues vendor RFQ. Fair-and-reasonable pricing established. Funding then flows via MILSTRIP, MIPR, or 7600B G-Invoicing. Not a GPC checkout workflow. Program was created specifically for orders exceeding the Government Purchase Card threshold.
  • Best buyer fitBuyers who prefer DLA in the middle of the transaction, or who are more comfortable with DoD-style ordering.
  • Speed expectationNot automatically faster than GSA. Can be slower due to additional gate steps. Value is in buyer confidence and execution lift.
  • Envision roleDirect revenue participation when the buy routes through TLS. Opens buyers and use cases not already converting through GSA.
CoverSix GSA MAS
Direct schedule ordering lane
  • Buyer experienceDirect schedule ordering under MAS procedures. Ordering activity manages evaluation and funds at its own level. Payment via normal ordering rules, including Government Purchase Card acceptance as a payment mechanism subject to agency restrictions. BPA structures available for repetitive demand.
  • Best buyer fitBuyers already comfortable with GSA Schedule tools and direct order processes. Repetitive demand suited for a BPA.
  • Speed expectationOften faster for ready buyers. Streamlined when demand is predictable and pricing is already agreed.
  • Envision roleIndirect value through supporting collaboration. Core sales lane controlled directly by CoverSix.
Routing Decision Rules
Use TLS first
DoD buyer prefers DLA-managed scope review before funding; requirement is in-scope; Envision relationship materially helps convert or process the buy.
Use GSA first
Buyer already prefers GSA Schedule; requirement is straightforward on CoverSix's MAS offering; repetitive direct orders or a BPA make sense.
Run both
Early qualification phase when buyer preference is not yet known; strategic account where the fastest compliant lane should win.
Do not force TLS
When the same buyer could already buy quickly via GSA MAS and Envision does not bring different access or execution value.
What Would Actually Increase Sales

Not every TLS routing decision creates new revenue. Here is the honest test.

Real growth and channel substitution are easy to confuse. The sections below draw the line clearly so partnership priorities stay honest.

Real Growth
Net-new buyer conversion
  • Envision helps convert DoD buyers who were not already buying through CoverSix's GSA MAS. DLA-assisted acquisition reduces buyer friction for customers who prefer a requisition-style workflow over direct schedule ordering. These are buyers CoverSix's GSA lane was not converting. This is where TLS creates net-new revenue.
Not Real Growth
Channel substitution
  • The same buyer routes the same sale through TLS instead of GSA. No new conversion, no larger order, no additional workshare. This is channel substitution dressed as growth. We do not count it as success and we do not pursue it as a priority.
Long-Term Growth Posture
The long-term growth posture is different. Envision first proves channel value on AKUA and selected kits, then expands into meaningful labor participation, and only later pursues a Procurement List case where the product architecture actually supports it. Real growth comes from new buyer conversion and deeper workshare, not from rerouting existing demand.
Revenue Pipeline

A five-phase collaborative pipeline, honestly sequenced.

Near-term revenue is real and achievable. The durable moat comes later. Treating the long-horizon AbilityOne pathway as a near-term sales tool creates bad incentives and weak positioning.

Phase 01 • Near-term
Prove the wedge
0 to 6 months
Identify target defense buyers and partner-led opportunities where AKUA can be sold now through the most practical route. Confirm TLS-in-scope use where available. Use GSA MAS where direct schedule buying is easier.
Outcome: AKUA pilots, small-batch orders, clean route-to-contract decisions.
Phase 02
Pilot, retrofit, repeat
3 to 12 months
Convert the lower-dollar device into repeatable deployments. Sell to existing and prospective users as pilots, retrofits, replacements, and small fleet fieldings. Emphasize tracking, tamper awareness, and in-transit accountability.
Outcome: Repeat orders and growing installed base.
Phase 03
Scale existing AKUA attach
6 to 18 months
Standardize quote language, ordering logic, retrofit packages, option descriptions, and route-to-contract decisions for the AKUA upgrade already sold on Lange assets. Commercial scaling of what already works.
Outcome: Higher attach rate. More retrofitted assets.
Phase 04
Expand workshare
12 to 24 months
Add meaningful Envision labor in final assembly, federal-specific configuration, provisioning, packaging, labeling, serialized asset prep, installation kits, and repeatable production touches that are real and auditable.
Outcome: Higher gross-margin participation. Foundation for long-horizon strategy.
Phase 05 • Long-horizon
Evaluate AbilityOne PL pathway
18 to 24+ months
Pursue a federal-specific, repeatable product candidate only after demand, standardized definition, and labor content are proven. Focus on standardizable smart-asset kits or configured subassemblies, not full custom hardened structures.
Outcome: Potential NSN designation, mandatory-source benefits, recurring federal demand moat.
Why the last phase is the last phase. The AbilityOne Procurement List is not a quick-turn commercialization tool. Suitability, NPA qualification, labor content, fair-market-price development, Federal Register notice, and Commission approval all take time. The right candidate is a standardized AKUA-enabled smart-asset kit, not a fully custom structure. Forcing a PL addition before the product is standardized, demand is visible, and Envision labor content is meaningful creates a weaker case, not a faster path.
